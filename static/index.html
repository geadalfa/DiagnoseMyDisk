How bad-sector repair actually works

• Mechanical HDD: A pending bad sector (SMART 0xC5) is resolved on the next write cycle. The drive firmware remaps the defective sector to a spare sector (SMART 0x05 Reallocated Sector).
• SSD (NVMe & SATA): Physically worn NAND cells cannot be repaired by software. Software can repair corrupt file-system clusters (MFT), verify partition integrity, and issue TRIM so the controller retires dead blocks and refreshes wear leveling.

File system repair (CHKDSK)

Scan and repair partition metadata, file indexes, and bad clusters online without a restart.

Panduan Edukasi Teknologi Flash NAND & Fitur SSD/Disk

Penjelasan mendalam tentang QLC, TLC, MLC, SLC, DRAM, HMB, TRIM & SMR

Flash cell hierarchy: SLC vs MLC vs TLC vs QLC

NAND flash stores electrons inside silicon transistors. The main difference between SLC, MLC, TLC, and QLC is how many data bits are packed into one physical cell:

Cell type Bits / cell Voltage states P/E endurance Characteristics & performance
SLC 1 Bit 2 (0/1) 50.000 - 100.000 Fastest, lowest latency, highest endurance. Most expensive. Used as cache.
MLC 2 Bit 4 (00..11) 3.000 - 10.000 Very fast and durable. Used in older Samsung Pro drives.
TLC (Standar) 3 Bit 8 1.500 - 3.000 The modern standard (ADATA Legend 900, 980 Pro). Balanced price, capacity, and multi-year endurance.
QLC 4 Bit 16 300 - 1.000 About 33% denser and much cheaper. Relies on SLC cache for everyday speed.

What is the point of QLC?

1. Why do manufacturers build QLC?
Fitting 4 bits into one cell (16 micro voltage states) raises capacity by about 33% on the same silicon wafer. That makes very large SSDs (2TB, 4TB, even 8TB) far more affordable for regular users.

2. Why does it need SLC cache?
Because the controller must resolve 16 tightly spaced voltage levels, direct-to-die QLC writes are slow (~80-150 MB/s). To hide that, the controller programs part of the free capacity as SLC cache (fast 1-bit writes). Everyday copies can hit 3,000-5,000 MB/s. Copy a very large file (say a 100GB game) until the cache fills, and speed drops to native QLC speed.

3. Does QLC wear out quickly?
Despite a ~300-1,000 P/E rating, a modern 2TB QLC SSD is usually rated for 400-800 TBW. For normal use and gaming (about 30-50 GB written per day), it can last well over 15-20 years before reaching its endurance limit.

DRAM cache vs DRAM-less (HMB)

• DRAM cache: A dedicated RAM chip on the SSD that stores the Flash Translation Layer (FTL) and speeds up random 4K lookups.
• HMB (Host Memory Buffer): A DRAM-less NVMe SSD can borrow about 64MB of system RAM over PCIe DMA to store the FTL. That brings modern DRAM-less drives close to the performance of drives with dedicated DRAM.

Why TRIM matters

TRIM tells the SSD which blocks Windows has deleted. That avoids slow read-modify-write cycles and keeps write amplification (WAF) low.

SMR vs CMR (hard disks)

SMR drives (such as the WD20EZAZ) overlap disk tracks to pack more capacity cheaply. They are stable for archives and backups, with a 256MB DRAM buffer.
